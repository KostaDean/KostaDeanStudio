Roots and Radicals

Roots undo exponents. They are opposite operations.

What is a root?

A root asks what number must be multiplied by itself a certain number of times to make another number.

If an = b, then n√b = a.

Roots and exponents are opposite operations, just like addition and subtraction or multiplication and division.

Square roots

The most common root is the square root.

If a2 = b, then √b = a.
4 = 22, so √4 = 2
25 = 52, so √25 = 5

Cube roots and index

The small number in front of the radical is called the index. It tells how many equal factors are used.

3√27 = 3 because 33 = 27.
Usually we do not write the small 2 for square roots. So √9 means 2√9.

Radical and radicand

The symbol √ is called a radical. The number under it is the radicand.

√49 = 7

About Math



About Math:
Every mathematical operation has its "inverse operation" (its opposite) that undoes what the operation did. The inverse of addition is subtraction. The inverse of multiplication is division. And so the inverse of exponents is radicals.

Notes to Parents



Notes to Parents:
Knowing the squares and square roots etc of some simple numbers helps in mentally locating numbers. 

Have your young learner know these simple squares.
2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64
3, 9, 27, 81, 
5, 25, 125
7, 49
8, 64
9, 81
10, 100, 1000

We can use these to get rough approximations of some other square or square roots. For example, the square root of 15 is between 3 and 5 since 3^2=9 and 5^2=25 and 15 is between 9 and 25.
Such estimates aim to acquire greater facility mentally placing numbers. But we should use calculators for greatest accuracy.
