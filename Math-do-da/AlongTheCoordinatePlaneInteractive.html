Along the Coordinate Plane

Learn how ordered pairs locate points using the x-axis and y-axis.

The coordinate plane

The coordinate plane consists of two number lines. The horizontal one is called the x-axis, and the vertical one is called the y-axis.

The point where the two axes meet is called the origin. Its coordinates are (0, 0).

Ordered pairs

Each point P on the plane is identified by an x-coordinate and a y-coordinate. We write them as an ordered pair:

P(x, y)

The x-coordinate  is how far horizontally from the Origin point P is. Positive x is to the right. Negative x is to the left.

The y-coordinate is how far vertically from the Origin point P is. Positive y is up from the Origin. Negative y is down from the Origin.

Examples

Example 1: Starting at (2, 5), go horizontally 3 steps and vertically 1 step.

(2 + 3, 5 + 1) = (5, 6)

Example 2: Starting at (−1, 3), go horizontally 5 steps and vertically −4 steps.

(−1 + 5, 3 − 4) = (4, −1)

About Math:
The use of coordinates marks a key moment in the development of Mathematics by the French philosopher/mathematician .René Descartes (1596–1650). It enables us to associate numbers with points. Joins Algebra with Geometry for the great advancement of both.

Notes to Parents



Notes to Parents:
These exercises aim at developing mental images, movement and placement of points and numbers along the Coordinate Plane. And the idea of movement along the Coordinate Plane: moving horizontally to the right is positive and moving to the left is negative. While moving vertically up is positive while moving down is negative.

Exercises

2) Starting at (−1, 3), go horizontally 5 steps and vertically −4 steps. Where are you?

4) Starting at (3, −2), go 5 steps left and 6 steps up. Where are you?

5) Starting at (−4, −3), go 2 steps right and 7 steps up. Where are you?

6) Starting at (6, 1), go 8 steps left and 3 steps down. Where are you?

7) Starting at (−2, 6), go 4 steps right and 9 steps down. Where are you?

8) Starting at (5, −5), go 5 steps left and 5 steps up. Where are you?
