Graphs of Equations

A solution to an equation with variables x and y gives matching values for x and y that make the equation true.

Solutions as points

Such values can be expressed as the coordinates  (x, y), of points on the coordinate plane.

All such points (x, y)  are said to form the graph of that equation.

Rule: The graph of an equation includes all the solutions to the equation.

Example: y = 2x + 1

Choose some values of x. Then calculate the corresponding values of y.

xy = 2x + 1point
01(0, 1)
13(1, 3)
25(2, 5)

The graph of this equation is a straight line.

Checking a point

To check if a point (x, y) is on the graph of the equation, substitute x and y into the equation. If the equation is satisfied the point is on the graph. If not satisfied the point is not on the graph.

Example: Is (1, 3) a solution of y = 2x + 1?

Substitute x = 1 and y = 3:

3 = 2(1) + 1 = 3 ✓

Yes, (1, 3) is a solution and is on the graph.

Notes to Parents



Notes to Parents:
Graphs are fundamental in Math. Many more advanced concepts are defined using graphs. A student can advance further in Math by being comfortable using graphs. So much information now in our lives use various graphs. It ia a visual and immediate means of communicating data and ideas.

About Math



About Math:
Every equation has a characteristic graph. Linear equation have graphs of straight lines while quadratic (second degree) equations, for example, graph as parabolas. Often we can know the type of equation we have by looking at its graph. The association between equations and graphs is very powerful and important in Math. As it connect Algebra and Geometry to the benefit of both.
