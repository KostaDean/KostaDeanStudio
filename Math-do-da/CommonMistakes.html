Common Mistakes

The forms of two quantities can look alike, but their values may not always equal.
Note: Two quantities are the same if they are always equal.

Changing form safely

A simple way to check whether two forms are the same is by substituting easy numbers and calculating the values.

If the values are different, then the forms cannot be the same quantity.

Important: Similar-looking forms are not necessarily equal.

Do not split sums, products, powers, roots, or denominators unless a valid rule allows it.

Example: a common fraction mistake

Is this true?

ab + c = ab + ac

Test with a = 2, b = 1, and c = 1.

21 + 1 = 22 = 1
21 + 21 = 2 + 2 = 4
1 ≠ 4

So the two forms are not the same quantity.

Some common mistakes

1) ab + c ≠ ab + ac

Do not split a denominator that is a sum.

2) a + bc + d ≠ ac + bd

A fraction with sums in top and bottom cannot be split this way.

3) (x + y)² ≠ x² + y²

Correct: (x + y)² = x² + 2xy + y².

4) √(a + b) ≠ √a + √b

A square root does not split over addition.

5) a + bcd ≠ ac + bd

Do not pair pieces of the numerator and denominator unless a valid rule says so.

Good habit

When unsure, test with easy numbers. One counterexample is enough to show two forms are not the same.

About Math



About Math:
A quantity is what can have numerical value. This is different from a quality, which often has human value and is very subjective. Quantities are objective. Mathematics concerns objective truths. These are what are true for all people at all times. While subjective believes can divide us, objective truths can unite us.
