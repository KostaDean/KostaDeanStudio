Roots and Radicals

Roots undo exponents. They are opposite operations.

What is a root?

A root asks what number must be multiplied by itself a certain number of times to make another number.

If an = b, then n√b = a.

Roots and exponents are opposite operations, just like addition and subtraction or multiplication and division.

Square roots

The most common root is the square root.

If a2 = b, then √b = a.
4 = 22, so √4 = 2
25 = 52, so √25 = 5

Cube roots and index

The small number in front of the radical is called the index. It tells how many equal factors are used.

3√27 = 3 because 33 = 27.
Usually we do not write the small 2 for square roots. So √9 means 2√9.

Radical and radicand

The symbol √ is called a radical. The number under it is the radicand.

√49 = 7
